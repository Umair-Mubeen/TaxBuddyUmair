Late Filing Penalty & ATL Surcharge Calculator — Pakistan 2026

⚖️ Section 182 & 182A ✅ Finance Act 2026 🧾 Form ATL-U option
Quick answer

A late income tax return in Pakistan attracts a Section 182 penalty of Rs 1,000 a day or 0.1% of tax payable a day (whichever is higher), with a minimum of Rs 10,000 for salaried individuals and Rs 50,000 for others, reduced by 75%, 50% or 25% if you file within one, two or three months. To get back on the ATL you also pay the Section 182A surcharge — Rs 25,000 (individual), Rs 50,000 (AOP), Rs 100,000 (company) — or, as an individual, file Form ATL-U instead.

How the Late Filing Penalty Is Calculated (Section 182)

Entry 1 of the Table in Section 182 of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001 applies where a person fails to file the return of income required under Section 114 by the due date. The penalty is the higher of 0.1% of the tax payable for each day of default, or Rs 1,000 for each day of default, followed by a minimum, a maximum and an early-filing reduction.

RuleAmount
Per day of defaultHigher of 0.1% of tax payable or Rs 1,000
Minimum — individual with 75%+ salary incomeRs 10,000
Minimum — all other casesRs 50,000
Maximum200% of tax payable
Filed within 1 / 2 / 3 months of the due dateReduced by 75% / 50% / 25%

"Tax payable" — after the Finance Act 2026 — is the higher of the tax chargeable on the assessed income for the year and the tax payable for the immediately preceding tax year for which a return was duly filed.

Worked Example

Mr. Bilal, a salaried individual, owes Rs 60,000 tax for Tax Year 2026. The due date was 31 October 2026 and he files on 20 November 2026 — 20 days late. Rs 1,000 × 20 = Rs 20,000 is higher than 0.1% × 60,000 × 20 = Rs 1,200, and above the Rs 10,000 minimum. He filed within one month, so the penalty is reduced by 75%: Rs 5,000. To get back on the ATL he either pays the Rs 25,000 surcharge or files Form ATL-U if he will not buy property for six months.

The ATL Surcharge — Section 182A

A person who files after the due date is not included in the Active Taxpayers List for that year (s.182A(1)(a)) unless he pays the surcharge, raised by the Finance Act 2026 to Rs 25,000 (individual), Rs 50,000 (AOP) and Rs 100,000 (company).

Under Section 182A(3) and Rule 81B(2B)–(2D) (S.R.O. 2097(I)/2026), an individual can instead furnish Form ATL-U on IRIS, undertaking not to purchase, acquire or obtain ownership or beneficial interest in any property for six months from the IRIS acknowledgement. If FBR finds a breach, the benefit is withdrawn. Full guide: ATL surcharge waiver via Form ATL-U.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the surcharge the same as the penalty?
No. The s.182A surcharge only puts you back on the ATL. The s.182 penalty for late filing is separate and is imposed by the Commissioner. Both can apply.

I have no tax payable. Do I still pay a penalty?
The minimum (Rs 10,000 salaried / Rs 50,000 others) still applies, reduced by 75%, 50% or 25% if you file within one, two or three months. Where 200% of tax payable is below the minimum, the law does not say which proviso prevails — this calculator uses the minimum as a cautious estimate.

Is there still a separate "late filer" withholding rate?
No. Rule 1A of the Tenth Schedule was omitted by the Finance Act 2026. Once you are on the ATL — by surcharge or ATL-U — you pay normal filer rates.

When will the ATL show my name?
FBR updates the ATL daily (Rule 81B(4)). Check with our ATL Check tool after paying the surcharge or filing ATL-U. See also: late filer to active filer.
