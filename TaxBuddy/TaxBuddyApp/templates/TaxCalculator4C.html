Section 4C Super Tax Calculator — with Imputable Income

ITO 2001 · Section 4C Individual / AOP Official format
Quick answer

Under Section 4C of the Income Tax Ordinance 2001, super-tax "income" is the sum of four parts: (A) profit on debt, dividend, capital gains and commission; (B) taxable income under section 9; (C) imputable income (your final-tax / PTR tax grossed up to income at normal rates); and (D) schedular income. Super tax is then charged on that total at the slab rate for your income band and tax year — for Tax Years 2023–2025 it ran from 0% (up to Rs 150 million) to 10% (above Rs 500 million); Tax Year 2026 cut the middle bands by 0.5 points; and from Tax Year 2027 (FY2026-27) most individuals and AOPs pay nil up to Rs 500 million and 8% above.

How Section 4C super tax & imputable income work

Section 4C of the Income Tax Ordinance 2001 charges an additional "super tax" on high-income persons. The income it is charged on is defined in section 4C(2) as the sum of: (i) profit on debt, dividend, capital gains and brokerage/commission; (ii) taxable income under section 9; (iii) imputable income; and (iv) income under the Fourth, Fifth, Seventh and Eighth Schedules.

Imputable income (section 2(28A)) is the income which, if taxed at normal rates, would have produced the same tax that was actually collected under the final/presumptive tax regime. This calculator grosses up the PTR tax you enter back into income using the non-salaried slab, so a large final-tax amount correctly adds a large income figure to the super-tax base.

Super tax is charged on the whole total income at the rate for the band it falls in. Section 4C was introduced by the Finance Act 2022. For Tax Years 2023–2025 the rate ran 0% up to Rs 150 million, then 1%, 2%, 3%, 4%, 6%, 8% and 10% above Rs 500 million. Tax Year 2026 (Finance Act 2025) cut the bands between Rs 200–500 million by 0.5 points. From Tax Year 2027 (FY2026-27, Finance Act 2026), most individuals and AOPs pay nil up to Rs 500 million and 8% above, while banking, oil & gas exploration and fertilizer sellers pay 10% above Rs 150 million. Imputable income — the gross-up formula

Imputable income (section 2(28A)) is the income which, if taxed at normal rates, would have produced the same tax that was collected under the final / presumptive regime (FTR/PTR). The final tax is grossed up to income using the non-salaried slab.

Final tax T → imputable income I:
  1. T ≤ 0 → I = 0
  2. T ≤ 90,000 → I = 600,000 + T ÷ 0.15
  3. T ≤ 170,000 → I = 1,200,000 + (T − 90,000) ÷ 0.20
  4. T ≤ 650,000 → I = 1,600,000 + (T − 170,000) ÷ 0.30
  5. T ≤ 1,610,000 → I = 3,200,000 + (T − 650,000) ÷ 0.40
  6. T > 1,610,000 → I = 5,600,000 + (T − 1,610,000) ÷ 0.45

The common shortcut I = (T − 1,610,000) ÷ 0.45 + 5,600,000 is only the last line — accurate when imputable income is in the top (45%) bracket. The non-salaried slab is year-specific (shown: TY2025).

How 4C income is built — Section 4C(2)

A — 4C(2)(i)
Profit on debt, dividend, capital gains, commission
B — 4C(2)(ii)
Taxable income under section 9
C — 4C(2)(iii)
Imputable income (grossed up as above)
D — 4C(2)(iv)
Schedular income (4th, 5th, 7th, 8th Schedules)

Total 4C income = A + B + C + D → Super tax = total × band rate.

Example (TY2025): A = 455,220,000; B = 2,000,000; imputable tax 40,409,020 → C = 91,820,044; D = 1,000,000. Total = 550,040,044 → 10% → super tax 55,004,004; less paid 700,000 → 54,304,004 payable.
