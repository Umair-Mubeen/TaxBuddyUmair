Do I Need to File an Income Tax Return? — Pakistan Filing Checker

⚖️ Section 114 & 115 ✅ Finance Act 2026 ⏱️ 1-minute check
Quick answer

You must file a return in Pakistan if your taxable income is above Rs 600,000 a year, or — whatever your income — if you hold an NTN, own 500 sq yd or more of land, a flat in municipal or cantonment limits, a car above 1000cc, a commercial or industrial electricity connection with an annual bill above Rs 500,000, or were charged to tax in either of the last two tax years (Section 114). Every company and NPO must always file.

Who Must File an Income Tax Return in Pakistan (Section 114)

Section 114(1) of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001 lists who must furnish a return of income for a tax year. Some persons must file because of what they are (a company, an NPO), some because of how much they earn, and many because of what they own or hold — even with little or no income.

ClauseWho must file
114(1)(a)Every company
114(1)(ab)Any other person whose taxable income exceeds the taxable limit (Rs 600,000 for individuals and AOPs)
114(1)(ac)Every non-profit organisation (s.2(36))
114(1)(ae)Every person with income subject to final taxation
114(1)(b)(i)–(ii)Charged to tax in either of the two preceding tax years; or claims a loss carried forward
114(1)(b)(iii)–(v)Owns land of 500 sq yd or more, or a flat in municipal / cantonment / ICT limits, or a flat of 2,000 sq ft or more in a rating area
114(1)(b)(vi)–(vii)Owns a motor vehicle above 1000cc; or has obtained an NTN
114(1)(b)(viii)Commercial or industrial electricity connection with an annual bill above Rs 500,000
114(1)(b)(ix)–(x)Resident registered with a chamber, trade body, market committee or professional body; resident individual required to file the foreign income and assets statement (s.116A)
114(1)(c)Persons or classes notified by the Board
114(1A)Individual with business income above Rs 300,000 up to Rs 400,000

Who Gets Relief — Section 115(3)

A widow, an orphan below 25 and a disabled person are not required to file solely because they own property or a vehicle above 1000cc (s.114(1)(b)(iii)–(vi)). A non-resident is not required to file solely because of owning immovable property. If any other condition applies — income above the limit, an NTN, final-tax income — the return is still required.

Worked Example

Ayesha earns a salary of Rs 45,000 a month (Rs 540,000 a year) — below the Rs 600,000 limit, so clause (ab) does not apply. But she drives a 1300cc car and got an NTN when she opened a business account. Both s.114(1)(b)(vi) and (vii) apply, so she must file a return and, as a resident individual, a wealth statement — even though her tax is nil.

Frequently Asked Questions

My employer already deducts tax. Do I still file?
Yes, if your taxable salary is above Rs 600,000 or any other condition applies. Withholding by the employer does not replace your own return.

What is the due date?
For individuals and AOPs, 30 September after the tax year ends (Tax Year 2026 was extended to 31 October 2026). Companies with a tax year ending 1 January–30 June file by 31 December. Missed it? Use our late filing penalty calculator.

I am not required to file. Should I file anyway?
Often yes. Filing puts you on the Active Taxpayers List, which means lower withholding on property, vehicles, bank profit and cash withdrawals. See: benefits of being a filer.

What happens if I don't file?
Failure to file attracts a penalty under Section 182 and you stay off the ATL. Check your status with our ATL Check tool.
